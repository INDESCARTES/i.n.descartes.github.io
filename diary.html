agatha and relatability

07 / 23 / 2026

Agatha Aldridge was created to be something of a grey slate — akin to an opaque window that absorbs light. That doesn't mean it was all that intentional when I was first laying down the basis of her motivations.

She was always meant to be a social climber. That was always supposed to be obvious when reading her motivations. But there had to be more to her than that. She morphed from well-meaning to relatively apathetic and vicious to others outside her goals. Though as we go through edits, I'm trying to hone that more and more.

I hope it comes across in the final edits.

I was really worried, because I wanted to figure out whether or not I should make her more relatable. So far, she's a clout chaser who hates her boring, albeit stable, life in Montana and goes to Boston University to chase her dreams as a micro influencer.

Influencers are going through a bit of a societal retcon, and aren't as desired and popular as they used to be.

However, I can't help but remind myself that she was inspired by a specific author and character — Richard Papen of The Secret History fame. Whom I would say is an entertaining person to watch, but is so annoying. It circles back to being entertaining as you watch them (Portia and Agatha) suffer.

I hope someone enjoys the character.

today i have to decide not to be Milo Winter

06 / 12 / 2026

Commissioning artwork is literally one of the most addicting things you can do. Seeing your characters come to life is so enthralling that I literally had to remind myself not to jump into it so rashly, and that I needed to wait at least a week so I could pay rent and bills.

Still, though — do other authors fantasize about seeing their characters in a different media? I'm kind of obsessed with seeing how others see my characters when given my descriptions, and how close it is to my actual vision.

the art of investing in your passion

04 / 25 / 2026

There's a quiet part of taking the path to authorship that many people whisper about in forums, but you have to specifically demand to know. Following your dreams is so expensive.

It's not even funny how many posts you see of successful authors finally deciding to let the public know how much they spent on their art, films, and novels. And it's always a number that makes me wince and shake my head.

It's not to say that I think spending that kind of money is a bad thing. If I'm going to work a job I don't particularly like, it's better to have that money go toward something I enjoy. Character art, dev and line editors, audio equipment, a stylish background.

At this point, I'm just listing things I really want. LOL.

The art of making a novel you can look at and be proud of relies on you being willing to sacrifice a lot for the fun of writing. Even things you don't think about — the novels that influence your writing style, the movies that inspire that one scene that looked so cinematic in your mind, the food you eat when you're on a writing binge. All that money masterfully blends together and becomes your art.

Isn't that so wonderful?

xoxo, descartes
